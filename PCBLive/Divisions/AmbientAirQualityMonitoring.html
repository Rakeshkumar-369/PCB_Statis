Ambient Air Quality Monitoring

Ambient air quality is being monitored by APPCB as a function laid down in the Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 & the amendments there of in the State of Andhra Pradesh ever since its inception.

Air pollutant, both indoor and outdoor, cause significant harm to health. Therefore, monitoring of pollutants is important for effective air quality management. The Government monitors air quality in different areas to find out how much pollution is in the air and make sure pollutant levels are meeting health - based air quality standards.

Knowing how much pollution is in the air in a certain area helps air quality agencies know when and how to take action to protect public health.

The air monitoring data helps to:

  • Determine if air quality is meeting national standards.
  • Determine the highest pollutant concentrations.
  • Understand how pollutants behave and their relationship with the weather.
  • validate pollution modelling, used to test 'what if' scenarios.
  • Forecast air quality
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of air pollution control programs.
  • Evaluate the effects of air pollution on public health.
  • Track the progress of plans for meeting air quality standard.
  • Determine air quality trends.
  • Develop responsible and cost-effective pollution control strategies and policy decisions.

National Air Quality Monitoring (NAMP)

Central Pollution Control Board is executing a nation-wide programme of ambient air quality monitoring known as National Air Quality Monitoring Programme (NAMP). The network consists of 804 operating stations covering 344 cities/towns in 28 states and 6 Union Territories of the country. Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board is operating 72 NAMP stations in the state.

The objectives of the NAMP are to determine status and trends of ambient air quality; to ascertain whether the prescribed ambient air quality standards are violated; to Identify Non-attainment Cities; to obtain the knowledge and understanding necessary for developing preventive and corrective measures and to understand the natural cleansing process undergoing in the environment through pollution dilution, dispersion, wind based movement, dry deposition, precipitation and chemical transformation of pollutants generated.

Under NAMP four air pollutants viz., Sulphur Dioxide (SO2), Oxides of Nitrogen as NO2, Respirable Suspended Particulate Matter (RSPM / PM10) and Fine Particulate Matter (PM2.5) have been identified for regular monitoring at all the locations. The monitoring of meteorological parameters such as wind speed and wind direction, relative humidity (RH) and temperature were also integrated with the monitoring of air quality.

The monitoring of pollutants is carried out for 24 hours (4-hourly sampling for gaseous pollutants and 8-hourly sampling for particulate matter) with a frequency of twice a week, to have one hundred and four (104) observations in a year. The monitoring is being carried out with the help of Central Pollution Control Board; CPCB co-ordinates with SPCBs to ensure the uniformity, consistency of air quality data and provides technical and financial support to them for operating the monitoring stations.

A network of 72 ambient air quality monitoring stations are in operation in the State under National Air Quality Monitoring Programme (NAMP), to assess air quality in District Headquarters, Cities & towns and more air quality stations are proposed for installation covering the entire State as per CPCB guidelines. The air quality data is uploaded in EAQDES portal of CPCB for dissemination of data. Based on the monitoring results and trends, Government of Andhra Pradesh will be able to take preventive measures to control air pollution from different sources.

Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS):

APPCB is operating 12 Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations installed in the State of Andhra Pradesh with APPCB funds along with the financial assistance sanctioned under Central Government schemes. The CAAQM Stations are established to monitor the ambient air quality continuously that generate instantaneous data for parameters viz., PM10, PM2.5, SO2, NO2, NH3, CO, O3 and Benzene in addition to meteorological data. More no. of CAAQM Stations is proposed for installation in the State as per CPCB guidelines.

Data generated from CAAQMS is linked to the web portals of APPCB, CPCB and is available on public domains. The continuous data is useful for studying the sources of pollution on minute-to-minute basis and to observe any abnormal changes in the air quality due to unforeseen incidents in the surrounding regions.

APPCB is also having one Mobile CAAQM Station located at Visakhapatnam to dispense in times of emergencies in and around Visakhapatnam region and also to study and evaluate sources of pollution in areas concerned. The Mobile CAAQM Station is equipped with analyzers for gaseous pollutants viz., SO2, NO2, NH3, CO, O3, BTX and can capture wide range of organic pollutants in addition to particulates and meteorological data.

Noise Level Monitoring

Noise is defined as unwanted sound. Sound, which pleases the listeners, is music and that which causes pain and annoyance is noise. At times, what is music for some can be noise for others. Section 2 (a) of the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 includes noise in the definition of 'air pollutant'. Section 2(a) air pollution means any solid, liquid or gaseous substance including noise present in the atmosphere such concentration as may be or tent to injurious to human beings or other living creatures or plants or property or environment. Central Government has notified the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 to control noise pollution in the Country.

Real-time Noise Monitoring Stations (RTNMS):

APPCB is operating 25 Real-time Noise Monitoring Stations installed in the State of Andhra Pradesh with APPCB funds along with the financial assistance sanctioned under Central Government schemes. The RTNM Stations are established to monitor the ambient air quality with respect to noise levels in towns & cities.

Mandate for Noise Pollution:

To plan a comprehensive programme for prevention, control, and abatement of noise pollution and to secure the execution thereof;

To advise the State Government on any matter concerning prevention, control, and abatement of noise pollution.

To collect and disseminate information related to noise pollution.

To collaborate with Central Pollution Control Board in programme related to prevention, control, and abatement of noise pollution; and

To inspect noise pollution control areas, assess quality of noise and to take steps for prevention, control and abatement of noise pollution in such areas.

Police Department, Transport Department, Municipal Administration Department and Pollution Control Board of Andhra Pradesh are the designated authorities to implement the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 as per the Environment, Forests, Science & Technology Department, Government of Andhra Pradesh, G.O. Ms. No. 172, dated 12.10.2010.

As per the directions of the Hon’ble NGT directions, dated 15.03.2019 in OA No. 681 of 2018, APPCB has formulated the Action Plan and communicated to the Police Department, Transport Department, Municipal Administration Department and Education Department for implementation to control noise pollution in the State

APPCB issued directions to the designated authorities viz., Police Department, Transport Department, Municipal Administration Department for implementation of the Compensation Regime for violation of the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 as endorsed by the Hon’ble National Green Tribunal.
